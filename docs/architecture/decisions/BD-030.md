# BD-030 — Referenced master data is retained; hard delete is limited to never-used records

**Status:** CONFIRMED

## Decision

CY Web applies a cross-module master-data lifecycle rule:

> A master record may be hard-deleted only when it has never been referenced by business/history data. Once it has been referenced, its identity and historical relationships must be retained; ordinary removal from active use is handled through the domain's inactive/archive/status mechanism rather than hard delete.

This rule exists to preserve relational integrity and historical traceability in the normalized D1 model.

## Customer

A Customer may be hard-deleted only when it has no business/history references, such as Visits, customer-item quotation history, sales work orders, Defect Reports, or other retained customer-linked facts.

Once referenced:

- the Customer identity must remain;
- historical child records must not become orphaned;
- `closed_business / 已歇業` remains a business relationship status, not deletion;
- if a separate archive/inactive mechanism is later needed for search/selection UX, it is distinct from deletion.

Legacy Desktop's current behavior of deleting only the `Customers` row while leaving related rows behind is not carried into CY Web.

## Item

A formal Item may be hard-deleted only if it has never been referenced by any retained transaction, quotation history, defect record, BOM/recipe, outsourcing record, or other business/history relation.

Once referenced, the Item identity remains. Removal from active selection is handled as inactive/retired status or through ERP-driven lifecycle semantics. This also aligns with SMART ERP ownership of the formal Item master and the item-number history rules.

## Contractor

Contractors with retained outsourcing/pricing/stock/history references must not be hard-deleted. Legacy already has an `inactive` concept; CY Web preserves that semantic direction while normalizing relationships by `contractor_id`.

An entirely unused contractor record created by mistake may be hard-deleted subject to permission/audit policy.

## Other master/reference entities

The same principle applies to other master/reference records where historical business rows point to them: hard delete is allowed only while the record is truly unused and no retained relationship depends on it.

For configurable lookup values, the previously confirmed rule remains: values already used by business data are normally inactivated rather than hard-deleted.

## Referential-integrity implication

The D1 schema and service layer must prevent deletion that would orphan retained business data. The application should perform explicit reference checks and use foreign-key/restrict semantics where practical rather than relying only on UI button state.

Hard deletion of an eligible never-used master record is still an auditable administrative operation under the platform's deletion/audit policy.

## Migration implication

Legacy rows must not be automatically merged or deleted merely because a master record appears inactive or old. Existing relationships are reconciled first. Orphan conditions found during migration are data-quality exceptions to review, not a reason to cascade-delete historical rows.
